The committed entry point emitted no lines while a session was running. At the end it emitted the
invalid-result line and summary. Its transcript dump preserves each message and tool event in order,
but carries no event or turn timestamps. Consequently these observations show the total time to the
first visible evaluator output and the last event before return, but cannot say how long any
individual turn or tool call took. They also cannot distinguish model deliberation from host/tool
latency inside each interval.

## Verdict

On a quiet machine, all three ordinary Grok sessions returned before the 300-second deadline; the
earlier timeouts did not recur. That does **not** establish valid completion. All three sessions
ended invalid when the host permission layer cancelled a call: Grep in `super-build`, Bash `env` in
`diagnose`, and a Bash inspection chain in `super-ship`. The assignment-led redirect repair is not
implicated by any of those refused calls.

The evidence therefore supports a narrower conclusion: quiet-machine execution removes the timeout
censoring and reveals permission cancellations, but ordinary Grok sessions still do not complete as
valid trials. The available output cannot locate where time was spent within the 143-, 158- and
67-second sessions because it exposes ordered events without timestamps.

Total host-reported cost was **$0.39468084**, below the approved $1.00 ceiling.

No instrument, runner, scorer, timeout, skill, policy or schema change was made.
